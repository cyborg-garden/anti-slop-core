# anti-slop games: the floor for games an agent builds

`anti-slop-core.md` is the floor for words. This file is the same floor for small games, the
kind an agent makes in an afternoon: what a player sees, does, feels and hears. Every line a
player reads still follows the core. Nothing here relaxes it.

The one idea carries over. A game reads as machine-made when nobody decided anything: the
picture is the average of every picture, the controls are every control at once, and the
builder judged its own work. You do not fix that by removing the tells below. You fix it by
deciding what the game is, then checking for what rushed in.

Each tell has a before and an after, because a rule you cannot show in two lines teaches
nothing (core, I5).

## G1. Surface tells: what the picture gives away

- A glossy storybook or cartoon render: shiny skin, big wet eyes, rounded everything.
- Even light everywhere, so nothing in the room matters more than anything else.
- Purple and black, bloom, lens flare, a glow that is its own object.
- Hands with extra or melted fingers. A face straight from an image model.
- Text baked into an image. Two art styles in one game. Cyan sci-fi buttons.
- A sunny room under a line that says it is the middle of the night.

> Before: a kitchen in noon light, a glowing lamp, a smiling parent at the stove.
> After: one low lamp, the parent's sleeves at the edge of the frame, the rest of the room going dark.

## G2. Design tells: what the game asks you to do

- Key and lock chains, collectible notes, an inventory in a three-minute game.
- Health bars and timers nobody asked for.
- A button that names the next job, so the button plays the game for you.
- Three control sets on one screen: a header, things to tap in the picture, a row of buttons.
- "Press E to interact." A tutorial pop-up. A fetch quest.
- An ending choice that changes one line and nothing you can see.

> Before: take the key, use the key, open the cupboard, take the letter.
> After: tap the letter in the picture. It opens.

## G3. Motion and feel tells: what moves, and how

- Sprites that slide instead of step. A character who holds a cup in one pose and a can in the next.
- Arms that come apart at the elbow. A hand sunk into a table.
- A picture that never moves while you wait, or one that never stops moving.
- Camera shake, screen flash, jump scares, bouncy pop-ups, confetti.

> Before: a scream, and a figure lunging at the camera.
> After: a third hand on the table beside yours, and no line about it.

## G4. Sound tells: what you hear

- A soft pad under everything. Stingers. Music before the player has touched anything.
- Loud by default. Sounds that start with a click instead of rising.
- A sound that explains the picture.

> Before: a swell of strings as the door closes.
> After: the door's latch, and a breath one beat longer than the last one.

## G5. Text and screen tells: what the game says

- Explaining the strange thing ("the floor is wet because the pipe leaks").
- Saying the theme out loud ("some things are worth carrying").
- Stat readouts on a feeling ("31% calm"). Two lines of story stacked at once.
- A hint, a button label and a story line all saying the same thing.
- The game's own build history on screen ("now with sound"). This is the core's C5, fix
  memorialization, in a game.

> Before: "The boat is wet. The table is dry."
> After: a wet paper boat on dry wood, and nothing written.

## G6. Process tells: how the game was made

- The builder grading its own pictures, and passing them.
- "It gets better each round", when each round adds a control and takes away a reason to play.
- A new art style to get past a note, instead of fixing what the note said.
- Polishing one object while the game stops being a game.
- The same object drawn differently in two scenes.

> Before: round twelve of the same scene, one more button.
> After: round two, one thing cut, and someone who has not seen it plays it.

## The check, after building

1. Say what the player does, wants, risks and how it ends, in one line each. If a line will
   not come, there is no game yet, and no picture will fix it.
2. Play it on a phone-sized screen without reading the code. Can you find what to press?
   Does pressing it change something? Does it end?
3. Hold it against the reference picture you meant to match: light first, then colour, then detail.
4. Read every line on screen against the core.
5. Let someone, or a different model, who did not build it have the last word.
